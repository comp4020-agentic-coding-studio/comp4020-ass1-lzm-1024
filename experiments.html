Severe-weather safety guide

When conditions change, your plan must change first.

Choose a condition to reveal the actions recommended by Australian road-safety authorities. If conditions become severe, delaying the trip or stopping safely is often the best decision.

The safest severe-weather drive may be the one you postpone.

Check before the wheels move.

  1. 01
    Check official warnings

    Review the forecast, road closures, storms, bushfires, hail, snow, dust and heavy fog before leaving.

  2. 02
    Check the vehicle

    Tyre tread, lights, windscreen, wipers and demister all matter when grip and visibility fall.

  3. 03
    Keep another plan

    Change the route, delay the trip or arrange another way to travel if conditions are unpredictable or severe.

Decision rule: If the conditions are beyond your ability or visibility, do not continue.

Wet roads increase stopping distance and the risk of aquaplaning.

Slow down and make every input smooth.

  1. 01
    Use low-beam lights and the demister

    Make the vehicle easier to see and keep the windscreen clear of condensation.

  2. 02
    Double the following time

    Queensland guidance recommends at least 4 seconds for a standard car in wet weather, compared with 2 seconds in good conditions.

  3. 03
    Avoid sudden control inputs

    Brake, accelerate and steer smoothly. Slow down and avoid cruise control to reduce aquaplaning risk.

Remember: The signed limit is a maximum for good conditions—not a target in heavy rain.

If you cannot see enough road to stop within it, you are travelling too fast.

Be visible without dazzling anyone.

  1. 01
    Slow down and use low beam

    High beam reflects from fog and can make it harder to see. Use permitted fog lights only while visibility is poor.

  2. 02
    Do not overtake in dust

    Keep windows closed and use air recirculation to reduce dust entering the cabin.

  3. 03
    Stop somewhere genuinely safe

    If visibility deteriorates, pull off the traffic lane in a safe place and wait for conditions to improve.

Do not: Stop suddenly in a live traffic lane simply because visibility has fallen.

You cannot reliably judge the depth, current, debris or road damage beneath floodwater.

If it is flooded, forget it.

  1. 01
    Stop before the water

    Never attempt to drive across a flooded road or bridge—even if the water appears calm or shallow.

  2. 02
    Turn around and find another route

    Follow closure signs and official directions. Waiting is safer than testing the crossing.

  3. 03
    Check the road after flooding

    Recently flooded surfaces and bridges may remain damaged, unstable or slippery.

Non-negotiable: Never drive, walk or ride through floodwater.

Strong wind reduces stability; ice and snow reduce tyre grip.

Create time, space and gentle movement.

  1. 01
    Follow warnings and advisory speeds

    Check Bureau of Meteorology warnings and road updates; delay travel during severe weather where possible.

  2. 02
    Reduce speed and increase the gap

    Brake early and gently, accelerate slowly and avoid abrupt steering on wet or icy surfaces.

  3. 03
    Expect instability

    Allow additional space around trucks, trailers and caravans, which can be more affected by wind and require longer stopping distances.

Exit strategy: If conditions worsen, pull over somewhere safe and wait rather than pushing on.

Official guidance used: NSW Government—poor conditions · Queensland Government—wet weather · Queensland Government—dusty conditions · Bureau of Meteorology—know your risk

General education only. Follow current warnings, road closures, emergency services and the road rules in your state or territory.
